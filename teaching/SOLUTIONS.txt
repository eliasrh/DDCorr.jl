DDCorr workshop • worked answers

1. Which phase times does the first Iceland trace contain?

In S1_009900/Z7.HRIM..HHZ.sac, KA is P_pred and KT0 is S_pred. The numeric
times used for P and S are A and T0, respectively. O gives the event origin
relative to the SAC clock. Subtract O from A to read the P time after origin.
The Iceland S1 catalogue contains event origins, locations and magnitudes.
The SAC producer calculated arrival times to centre the windows using 6.0 km/s
for P and 3.5 km/s for S. On another dataset, inspect the field names and labels.

2. What do the Iceland baseline counts mean?

100 event folders × 11 stations × 3 components = 3,300 SAC traces.
Twelve stations met the original metadata selection; STJA had no usable
waveforms in the selected archive. The baseline produced 2,412 P rows
and 4,625 S rows, or 7,037 station–phase measurements across 1,914
distinct earthquake pairs. Separate stations can measure the same pair.
Multiple horizontal components can contribute to one S measurement.

In group_summary.csv, Z7.DYSA P had 66 prepared events. Its 2,145 possible
pairs yielded 800 accepted, 1,307 below the correlation threshold and 38 at
the search boundary. A boundary peak suggests the true optimum might lie
beyond the chosen lag range. Check the waveforms before increasing that range.

3. Does every convincing correlation demonstrate two different earthquakes?

In the existing [plots] section of a copy of Iceland100/DDCorr.toml, set
  event_pairs = [[44, 45], [28, 45]]
  example_stations = ["Z7.RODG", "Z7.STAM"]
  example_phases = ["S"]
Save this TOML in Iceland100/. Run DDCorr with this copy and open the printed
report. Use example_selection.csv to locate the requested figures.

IDs 28, 44 and 45 map to S1_009927, S1_009943 and S1_009944.
For 44/45, Z7.RODG S gives r = 0.99982 and lag = +0.0003 s. The two traces
nearly cover each other. Their catalogue origins differ by only 0.060 s and
15 accepted measurements at 11 stations have r between 0.908 and 0.99982.
Check the cuts in absolute UTC against the continuous record. Two catalogue
entries may be using the very same physical arrival. A high r warrants that
check; a decision to merge requires evidence of one source occurrence.

For 28/45, Z7.STAM S gives r = 0.700006 and lag = −0.0039 s. An early pulse
aligns better than the later coda. Compare the other horizontal component,
neighbouring lag peaks and the other stations. There are eight accepted
measurements across six stations for this pair; the strongest has r = 0.92963.

4. What happens when only the P threshold changes?

A Threshold.toml alongside the Iceland baseline with [P] min_cc = 0.90 leaves
233 P rows. All 4,625 S rows still pass. Together these give 4,858 observations
on 1,260 pairs. At 0.70 there were 2,412 P rows and 1,914 linked pairs. These
counts can also be checked by filtering the baseline correlations.csv on
cc_signed; the default comparison keeps r greater than or equal to the
threshold. The smaller set gives fewer P constraints for relocation.

5. How do we check the sign and error independently?

In the optional synthetic dataset, output events 1 and 2 map to original names
1201 and 17. At XX.S01 P their supplied pick-minus-origin times differ by
+0.3600 s (event 1 minus event 2). The waveform of event 2 arrives +0.2247 s
later relative to its own pick on the aligned axes. Subtract that correction.
DDCorr writes 0.3600 − 0.2247 = +0.1353 s for event 1 minus event 2. The
synthetic pulses were generated with a difference of +0.1350 s, giving a
signed error of +0.0003 s. The squared coefficient in dt.cc, approximately
0.9698, is the weight. Use check_synthetic.py as shown in START_HERE for all
accepted rows. Expect 80 signal measurements, maximum absolute error about
0.0006 s, and zero accepted rows for the noise-only event, original ID 905.

6. Which Friday result should we bring to DDSync and GraphSplit on Saturday?

Choose the 100-event baseline result and keep its dt.cc, catalog.txt,
stations.txt and event_id_map.csv together. In Iceland100/ use prepare_run.jl
with that result path or with reference, as described in START.txt. DDSync
uses alternate paths through the station–phase graph to test consistency.
GraphSplit uses measurements from several stations and a velocity model to
estimate relative positions. DDSync retains 6,736 of 7,037 observations in
the supplied run. GraphSplit uses 65 events for prelocation and 57 in the DD
solve. Select the 57 IDs in catalog_dd_filt.txt from each of the three
catalogues before comparing them. Iceland100/solutions/RESULTS.txt and matched_locations.png give the
worked comparison. Assess coverage, link redundancy and station geometry
before interpreting a change in cluster shape.


7. Trace events 28 and 45 through Saturday’s relocation.

Both IDs appear in graphsplit_output/catalog_dd_filt.txt. Match the final
integer ID in each catalogue to the same two IDs at input and prelocation.
Their horizontal separation is about 0.176 km initially, 0.171 km after
prelocation and 0.172 km after DD. Their 3-D separation is about 0.291,
0.355 and 0.362 km. Most of the change for this pair was in the vertical
direction. These are distances between the fitted hypocentres.

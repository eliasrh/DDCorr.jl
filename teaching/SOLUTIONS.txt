Reading the Bárðarbunga DDCorr report

The Iceland input

Iceland100/DDCorr.toml selects the SAC directory beside the settings file.
It defines the numerical SAC fields A for P and T0 for S. For example, the
vertical recording in S1_009992/Z7.DYSA..HHZ.sac has A = 3.991571 s and
T0 = 6.842693 s. Both times are measured from the reference instant in
the SAC header. In this archive it coincides with this event's origin.
Their labels P_pred and S_pred identify predicted phase times.

The metadata viewer prints a trace's channel, reference time, origin,
coordinates, orientation, sampling interval and phase markers. The
“Your own SAC files” section in Start Here gives the command for this
exact recording. The Iceland P window extends from 0.5 s before to 2 s
after its P marker. Both phases use a 2–14 Hz bandpass.

The Iceland report

Open report.html in your Iceland correlation folder, or use
Iceland100/reference/report.html for the supplied result. The catalogue
contains 100 events. The run retained 2,412 P measurements and 4,625 S
measurements across 1,914 event pairs. The observation count includes
separate measurements from every contributing station and phase.

The six “Quality and connectivity” panels

Upper left — All measured peaks. At each station and phase DDCorr searches
a correlation curve for its highest sampled coefficient. The step curves
count those values in bins of 0.05. Blue represents P and orange S. The
dashed lines at 0.70 are the configured limits for this Iceland run. Most
peaks fall below them. A pair without enough shared components has no
correlation curve to contribute.

Upper right — Accepted coefficients. This panel uses fitted coefficients for the rows retained
in correlations.csv, in bins of 0.025. The horizontal scale is signed r.
A concentration near the threshold can be investigated by viewing some
of those actual waveforms. The upper-left and upper-right graphs have
different vertical scales and bin widths. Exact acceptance and rejection
counts are in the group decisions table.

Middle left — Accepted residual lags. Each observation contributes its
fitted shift, in seconds, relative to the two input markers. Most Iceland
shifts cluster near zero. A positive value moves the second trace earlier
in the alignment plot. A concentration well to one side is worth checking
by station and phase for a marker convention or onset mismatch. The figure
contains accepted observations, so boundary rejections are found in the
pair-outcome panel and table.

Middle right — Event graph degree. For each event, count its distinct
partners across all stations and phases. Measuring a pair at several
stations adds one partner. The vertical scale counts events in the degree
range. Four Iceland events have zero partners. The component sizes above
the figures are 96, 1, 1, 1 and 1. A component means there is a path between
its members. event_connectivity.csv gives the degree of each event.

Lower left — Accepted observations by station. Each station has a P bar and
an S bar. Their heights are numbers of accepted station–phase measurements.
The two phases use different components, markers and signal windows, so
their coverage can differ at the same site.

Lower right — Pair decisions. Each bar counts an outcome after individual
traces have been prepared. For this run 7,037 comparisons were accepted,
50,361 fell below the threshold, 893 peaked on the search boundary, and
124 had insufficient common components. group_summary.csv divides the
outcomes by station and phase. preprocessing_issues.csv gives the earlier
reasons why individual traces failed preparation.

One DYSA P measurement

Under “Accepted pair examples”, the first DYSA P figure shows catalogue
IDs 93 and 97. The header of the figure and the entries in event_id_map.csv
identify the original folders as S1_009992 and S1_009996. The contributing
component is HHZ at station Z7.DYSA.

In the top panel the main pulse in event 97 is later on its P-marker-centred
axis. In the middle panel its waveform is shifted 0.0586 s earlier. The
strong positive peaks align. The following oscillations differ in detail.
In the bottom panel, the highest sampled coefficient is 0.70003 at
+0.060 s. A parabola fitted through that sample and its two neighbours
gives a lag of +0.0586 s.

The “Correlation audit” link under “Files and conventions” opens
correlations.csv. For events 93 and 97 at Z7.DYSA, phase P, the values are:

    lag_B_later_s    +0.0586 s   Shift relative to the input phase markers.
    cc_signed         0.704588  Waveform correlation at the fitted lag.
    dt_s             +0.2972 s   Estimated P travel time for 93 minus 97.
    weight            0.496445  Squared coefficient in this configuration.

The P markers are 3.9916 and 3.6358 s after the two event origins. Their
difference is +0.3558 s. Subtracting the fitted lag of +0.0586 s gives
a differential travel time of +0.2972 s for event 93 minus event 97.
The dt.cc block for this pair reads:

    # 93 97 0.0
    Z7.DYSA       0.2972 0.496445 P

A pair measured at several stations or phases has several observation lines
under the same header. Catalogue IDs 93 and 97 match the IDs in catalog.txt,
and station Z7.DYSA appears in stations.txt.

When the east and north components disagree

At STAM for events 28/45, east has r = 0.673144 and north has r = 0.726868 at the same zero-lag sample. Their mean of 0.700006 passes the 0.70 S cutoff. At the fitted lag of −3.86 ms the mean is 0.694417, giving a weight of 0.482215. The report shows each channel’s coefficients and preferred lag for selected S examples. To choose this one, use [plots] event_pairs = [[28,45]], example_stations = ["Z7.STAM"] and example_phases = ["S"].

Of the 3,973 accepted S observations using two horizontals, 736 have one component below 0.70 at the common sampled lag. The two channels choose the same grid point in 3,598 cases. In the remaining 375, the individual winners differ by one 20 ms sample. The maximum tolerated spread in the input pick-based differences is 1 ms. The code leaves independent waveform peak separation and coefficient differences to the reader to assess.

Station matrices and missing measurements

Under “Station/phase matrices (PNG)”, expand the Z7.DYSA P entry. For each
pair of event IDs the three panels show the signed coefficient, differential
time and residual lag. Grey cells have no accepted measurement. The
coefficient is mirrored across the diagonal. Use the smaller ID first
when looking up a row in correlations.csv. Reversing the pair order
reverses the sign of the lag and differential time.

For DYSA P, row 93, column 97 has coefficient 0.704588 in the left
matrix. The middle matrix gives +0.2972 s in that cell and −0.2972 s
in the reflected cell. The right matrix instead gives +0.0586 s, the
shift on the two marker-centred axes. Each time panel has its own colour
scale in seconds. Rows or columns with few coloured cells have few
partners measured at this station and phase.

Event 2 illustrates why a phase can be missing. Its Z7.DYSA vertical
file was present and the phase_readiness.csv table marks its P window
as ready. The filtered P signal was too weak relative to the pre-P noise;
preprocessing_issues.csv gives that reason. The two horizontal components
still supplied an S observation for events 1 and 2. The “Quality and
connectivity” section links the preparation tables; “Group decisions”
gives the totals for each station and phase.

Cycle consistency

For one station and phase, a triangle requires accepted differential times
for all three pairs among three events. The horizontal quantity is
dt(i,j) + dt(j,k) − dt(i,k) in seconds. A value near zero means the indirect
timing difference through j agrees with the direct difference. The vertical
scale counts triangles. The report pools P groups on the left and S groups
on the right, with separate vertical scales. Titles give sampled counts
and the number outside the ±0.5 s display.

Many Iceland values lie inside the central 0.01 s bins. The linked
triangle_closure_summary.csv resolves smaller differences by station and
phase. At DYSA, 90% of the absolute P mismatches are at or below 1.48 ms,
and 90% of S mismatches are at or below 1.97 ms. A broader tail indicates
inconsistent pair times. Closure by itself cannot identify the wrong edge;
a consistent bias on one event or a correlated noise pulse can still close.

Filter audit

The upper panels show amplitude gain against frequency in Hz for the P
and S settings applied to this dataset. A gain of one preserves a tone’s
amplitude. Both filters use 2–14 Hz; after filtering forward and backward,
the gain is 0.5 at 2 Hz and 14 Hz. The continuous curves are calculated
from the actual filter. The dots are measured by passing individual tones
through that filter. Exact gains appear in filter_tone_audit.csv.

The lower panels use real SAC recordings at TOHR: event S1_009943 in the
P panel and S1_009910 in the S panel. At each frequency, the grey line is
the raw power spectral density, and the coloured line is the filtered
density at the original sample rate. PSD measures power per unit frequency.
Both curves in each panel use the same raw peak as 0 dB. At any one frequency
a 10 dB drop means ten times less power density. The drop outside the
passband shows how the filter suppresses those frequencies. The two event
panels each have their own raw reference.

Near-identical recordings

The “Near-identical arrival flags” link near the top of the report opens
near_identity.csv. Of the 61 flagged observations, the row for events
44 and 45 at Z7.RODG, phase S, has fitted r = 0.999980. Under “Accepted pair
examples”, example_09_Z7.RODG_S shows the marker-centred alignment.
The slide adds a comparison on a common UTC clock, made directly from
the two original east-component SAC recordings. All 9,495 overlapping
samples match. DDCorr retains both catalogue IDs for review.

Report switches in the TOML

Choosing the traces in the filter spectrum

The lower filter panels use the lower-ID event and first shared component of the last eligible example for each phase, before max_examples limits the gallery. To choose an accepted trace, set one event_pairs pair, one example_stations station and one example_phases phase. For STAM S, [[85, 86]], ["Z7.STAM"] and ["S"] select event 85. These selectors affect the gallery too.

When only the P band was narrowed from 2–14 to 4–8 Hz, 4,626 P measurements passed versus 2,412. There were 1,103 ambiguous_peak rejections versus zero. A narrow passband can make separate cycles look alike. For KVER events 13/27 the two narrow-band peaks are about 160 ms apart. The 1,945 P measurements accepted in both runs differed by at most 19.1 ms; the newly accepted rows do not have that broad-band comparison.

With [plots] enabled = true, summary, closure, filters and examples select
the summary charts, cycle-consistency histograms, filter audit and gallery.
max_examples limits the automatic gallery. [matrices] enabled and plots
produce the station/phase images within the configured size limits.
[matrices] dense controls separate dense CSV tables; the PNGs can be
produced with dense = false.

What to keep

Save the whole correlation directory to keep the browser report, figures,
measurements and effective configuration together. For relocation, use
dt.cc, catalog.txt, stations.txt and event_id_map.csv from that same run.
